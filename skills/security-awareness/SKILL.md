# Security Awareness

## When to use

- Develop role-based awareness learning and policy communications
- Build tailored learning paths from approved role and skill-gap assessments
- Measure knowledge retention and improve security culture
- Plan an authorized phishing or social-engineering simulation

## Procedure

1. Define learning objectives, audiences, accessibility needs, and success measures.
2. Use short, practical scenarios tied to real work; include reporting and recovery actions.
3. Get written approval for simulations, define recipients, dates, safeguards, and an opt-out/escalation contact.
4. Use benign landing pages and synthetic data; measure aggregate outcomes rather than collecting passwords or private messages.
5. Avoid shame, public ranking, deceptive high-consequence lures, and punitive metrics.
6. Report participation and learning trends in aggregate; update training paths based on measured gaps.

## Safety boundaries

- No credential harvesting, malware delivery, impersonation of emergency services, or simulation outside the approved population.
- Do not publish identifiable employee results without a documented, approved need.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Training has measurable objectives and accessible material.
- [ ] Simulations have written scope and do not collect credentials.
- [ ] Results are privacy-preserving and lead to actionable improvements.
